Solution

Start with what is actually playing.

Audex Trace is for Apple Music listeners who want to understand current playback, playback quality suggestions, output matching, and upcoming queue quality before the next track arrives.

AR

Actual Playback Readout

In Trace Pro, open Playback details to inspect the detected codec, bit depth, sample rate, and channels.

OM

Output Match Status

In Trace Pro, see the output device format, its current and supported sample rates, and whether it matches detected playback.

SR

Auto Sample Rate Match

Trace Pro switches supported output devices to match what is playing. You can optionally include lossy and Dolby playback.

Where Trace lives

Trace is a player as well as a monitor, so it can stay open while you work.

  • Player Window: a floating mini player that stays above your other windows, optionally over full-screen apps, with the current artwork as a blurred background if you want it. Horizontal trackpad swipes can move to the previous or next track.
  • Menu Bar: current track, controls, volume, and the Playing Next queue without leaving what you are doing.
  • Notch: the same track and controls in the display cutout, expanded only when you want them.

The views are all in the free version. The playback details shown inside them are Pro.

Quality before the track starts

Playing Next shows what is queued. Trace estimates the quality of those tracks before they play: Lossless, Hi-Res Lossless, or Dolby, with a best effort bit depth and sample rate where the catalog exposes it.

Apple Music tells you about the track you are hearing. Trace also tells you about the ones after it.

Tracks that look likely to fail or skip are marked, so a gap in the queue is not a surprise.

The queue itself is free. Quality estimates and skip-risk warnings are Pro. These are catalog estimates, not confirmed playback, and Trace labels them that way.

A better version may already be in the catalog

Apple Music can play a lower quality version of a track while a higher resolution release of the same recording sits in the catalog. Apple Music does not show you that comparison while you are listening to the current release.

While a track is playing, Trace checks for alternate releases. When it finds one, it shows what it found and can open it in Music. It suggests. It does not switch for you.

The catalog can change after you save music. A Lossless or Dolby Atmos version can appear on a release you never opened, and you would have no reason to check again.

Playback quality suggestions are a Pro feature. Availability is a catalog estimate, so Trace tells you what it found rather than what Music will play.

SK

Skip Risk Detection

In Trace Pro, get warnings for queued tracks that may fail, become unavailable, or interrupt playback, before they reach the player.
