Already using LosslessSwitcher or SyncHz?

Both are free, and both switch your output to the sample rate Apple Music is playing. If that is all you want, keep using them. Trace Pro is for people who also want the things below.

The readout and the switch follow the track you hear. Music prepares the next track before the current one ends, and that track logs its format early. This happens with Song Transitions, AutoMix, and Crossfade off too. A switcher that applies the newest log line can move your DAC to the next track's rate partway through a song, as two open LosslessSwitcher issues report (#159, #231). Trace keeps the readout and Auto Sample Rate Match on the track that is playing until the next one starts, on a best-effort basis. Within a track the readout is live: if Music's stream drops to a lower quality partway through a song, or comes back up, the readout changes when that happens.

Song Transitions, AutoMix, and Crossfade can stay on. The readout still changes when the next track starts. Auto Sample Rate Match waits until Music has finished handing over from the previous track, because changing the rate mid-blend makes Music cut the blend off and start it again. So the switch can land a few seconds after the track change, up to about 20 seconds in the worst case, and macOS resamples the new track until then. The developer tested this with AutoMix on macOS 26.

You see the whole format. Playback details show the codec, bit depth, sample rate, and channels of what is playing, next to your output device's current and supported sample rates and whether they match.

Playing Next shows what comes after this track. It lists up to 99 upcoming tracks with artwork, an estimated format for each, and a warning when one may skip. You can drag a track to move it, swipe to remove it, or double-click to play it.

Where Trace lives

Trace is also a player, so it can stay open while you work.

  • Player Window: a floating mini player that stays above your other windows, optionally over full-screen apps, with the current artwork as a blurred background if you want it. Horizontal trackpad swipes can move to the previous or next track.
  • Menu Bar: current track, controls, and volume, without leaving what you are doing.
  • Notch: the same track and controls in the display cutout, expanded only when you want them.

The Player Window, Menu Bar, and Notch are in the free version, with Now Playing, playback controls, track metadata, and artwork. Playing Next and the playback details shown inside the views are Pro.

A higher-resolution release may already be in the catalog

Apple Music does not always play the highest-resolution release of a recording. The same album can sit in the catalog at a higher bit depth or sample rate, or in Dolby Atmos, and Music does not mention it while you listen to the release you picked.

While a track is playing, Trace checks for alternate releases. When it finds one, it shows what it found and can open it in Music, but it does not switch playback for you.

The catalog also changes after you save music. A Lossless or Dolby Atmos version can appear on a release you never opened again.

Playback quality suggestions are a Pro feature. Availability is a catalog estimate: Trace tells you what it found, which is not always what Music will play.
