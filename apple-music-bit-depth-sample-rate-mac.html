Apple Music on Mac

How to check Apple Music bit depth and sample rate on Mac.

Apple Music's Lossless label is useful, but it is not the same as seeing the detected playback format and the current Mac output format side by side.

Updated June 27, 2026•For Apple Music on macOS

On this page

Quick answer What to check Steps Limits How Trace helps FAQ
Quick answer

Check playback and output separately.

The useful answer is to check two things separately: what Apple Music is playing now, and what macOS is outputting now. Apple Music may show a Lossless or Hi-Res Lossless label, but that label is not the same as a live readout of codec, bit depth, sample rate, and output match.

Short answer:

Use Apple Music settings and track labels to confirm availability, Audio MIDI Setup to see the selected Mac output format, and Audex Trace to see detected codec, bit depth, sample rate, and output match side by side.

What to check

The four numbers and labels worth separating.

SignalWhat it tells youWhy it matters
CodecThe detected current playback format, such as AAC or ALAC.Lossless Apple Music playback is expected to use ALAC, not AAC.
Bit depthThe detected current playback bit depth, such as 16-bit or 24-bit.Hi-Res Lossless can be 24-bit, but not every track is Hi-Res.
Playback sample rateThe detected current Apple Music playback sample rate.Your queue can move between 44.1, 48, 96, and 192 kHz tracks.
Mac output sample rateThe current output format selected for the active macOS audio device.It may stay fixed even when Apple Music playback changes.
Steps

A practical check before you change anything.

  1. Enable Lossless in Apple Music settings.Confirm streaming and download quality are set the way you expect.
  2. Play a known Lossless or Hi-Res Lossless track.Use a track where the Apple Music label appears clearly.
  3. Open Audio MIDI Setup.Check the Format menu for the active output device.
  4. Compare detected playback with Mac output.Audex Trace shows the current playback format and Mac output sample rate together.
Limits

A label is useful, but it is not the whole playback path.

Apple Music labels help you understand track availability and the selected quality tier, but a label alone does not tell you every layer of the playback path. It does not replace checking the current output device format in Audio MIDI Setup, and it does not guarantee bit-perfect output.

That is why Trace focuses on visibility: current playback, output match, and queue context are shown separately instead of being flattened into one badge.
